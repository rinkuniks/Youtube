# YouTube Shorts: what to make and how money arrives by December 2026

Researched 12 Oct 2026. Numbers come from the sources at the bottom. Income figures are my estimates, not guarantees.

## The honest bottom line

- **Ad revenue from Shorts by December is unlikely, and small even if you get it.** Full monetization needs **1,000 subscribers + 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days**. Shorts pay out of a shared pool at roughly **$0.02–$1.50 per 1,000 views** (tech/education sit at the higher end). So 10M views is worth only about **$500–$3,000**.
- **The rules get harder on 1 Feb 2027.** The Shorts entry bar doubles to **20M views in 90 days**, and every partner must keep **10M views every 90 days** to stay in the Shorts revenue pool. If you want ad revenue at all, get in before 31 Jan 2027. Existing partners keep their status.
- **The early tier is reachable:** **500 subs + 3M Shorts views in 90 days + 3 uploads**. That unlocks memberships, Super Thanks, brand deals through Creator Partnerships, and the **YouTube Shopping affiliate program** (500+ subs inside YPP; live in the US, India, Brazil, Japan and other markets).
- **The money that can realistically land by December comes from outside YouTube's ad system.** You can sell your AR/CV demos as products, take paid commissions from agencies and brands, and use affiliate links. YouTube is the shop window that brings buyers in.

## Your edge

Most of what goes viral in October 2026 is visual and needs no words to understand: illusions and reveals, satisfying loops, physical comedy, Halloween transformations, and quick product demos. Most AI Shorts are generic, faceless slideshows. A real hand-tracked squishy dough blob or an AR creature walking around your desk looks impossible, so people loop it and ask "how?". Very few creators can make that. Agencies and brands also pay for exactly this skill.

## Ranked niches

### 1. "Impossible tech" AR / hand-tracking demos (best bet)
Silent or music-only clips, 10–25 s, payoff in the first second, ending that loops back to the start.
- I punch/stretch/split a squishy AR dough blob with my bare hands (make a series: "day 1 vs day 30")
- My AR creature reacts to my cat / falls off my desk / gets scared of a real spider
- **Halloween now:** an AR ghost in my hallway, my face turned into a pumpkin with CV, hand tracking that summons fire
- **December:** AR snow that piles up on real furniture, a hand-tracked Christmas tree you can decorate
- "Fake or real?" comment bait: show the AR first, then reveal the rig

**Money:** sell the Unity project or template ($9–$49 on Gumroad or itch.io), take agency/brand commissions for AR filters or experiential demos, and pin "DM for commissions" in your channel and bio.

### 2. "I built this with AI in 60 seconds" (build-in-public dev Shorts)
Screen recording sped up, then the finished result. This fits the 60–90 s mini-tutorial sweet spot. Tech/education RPM is higher.
- Building a hand-gesture-controlled game in 1 hour using Claude
- Turning my webcam into a theremin
- Making a Snapchat-style filter from scratch with no SDK
- Answering "how did you make the dough blob?" as a reply Short to your own niche #1 videos
**Money:** affiliate links to dev and AI tools (many pay 20–30% recurring commission), a paid starter kit or course, and a funnel into long-form tutorials, which earn about 10–30× more per view.

### 3. Playable CV challenges ("can you beat my AI?")
- This AI judges your rock-paper-scissors faster than you can blink
- Only 1% can hold this pose long enough for the AI
- A Halloween "don't move" game with a jump scare when CV catches you blinking
Comments and duets push the algorithm. Link to a free browser version of the game.
**Money:** a "pay what you want" link on the browser game, sponsorships from webcam, AI or edtech brands, and later Shopping affiliate.

**Recommendation:** run #1 as the main channel and mix in #2 about 1 time in 4. Keep #3 for seasonal spikes (Halloween, Christmas). Keep everything on one channel so the subscribers all count toward the same thresholds.

## Posting plan (12 Oct – 31 Dec, about 11 weeks)

- **Cadence:** 1 Short a day, 2 a day once a format proves itself. That's about 90–110 Shorts total.
- **Weekly batch:** record 7–10 raw AR captures in one session, then edit and schedule them all.
- **Format rules:** hook frame at 0 s, 10–35 s length, burned-in captions, a looping ending, no slow intro. Watch "viewed vs. swiped away" in Analytics and keep the formats that get **≥70% viewed**.
- **Weeks 1–3 (to ~2 Nov):** test across all three niches and post Halloween content hard until 31 Oct.
- **Weeks 4–7 (Nov):** double down on the top 2 formats, start reply-to-comment Shorts, and launch the paid template.
- **Weeks 8–11 (Dec):** Christmas/winter AR series, and pitch brands with your view numbers.
- **Repost** the same clips to Instagram Reels and TikTok. That's free extra reach and more sponsor leverage.

## Realistic income timeline (estimates)

| When | What can pay you | Rough range |
|---|---|---|
| Oct (week 1) | Gumroad/itch page live, commission link in bio | $0 to first sale |
| Late Oct – Nov | Template sales, first agency/brand AR commission (one viral demo is enough to get noticed) | $50–$1,500 |
| Nov – Dec | Recurring template sales, 1–3 commissions/sponsors, affiliate | $300–$3,000+/mo if one format breaks out |
| Dec – Jan (if 500 subs + 3M views) | YPP early tier: Shopping affiliate, Super Thanks, Creator Partnerships | small at first |
| Before 31 Jan 2027 (if 1k subs + 10M views) | Shorts ad share | ~$50–$300/mo at that volume |

Your **first dollars by December most likely come from a commission or product sale, not AdSense.** One AR demo with 1M+ views is a portfolio piece agencies will pay for, and that matches the creative-agency AI work you already do.
